Toledo and Bowling Green have played 90 times since 1919. The series is **43-43-4**. Friday night at the Glass Bowl, somebody takes the lead.

Before kickoff, a captain calls the coin. Almost every winner says the same two words: *"We'll defer."* The idea is the **Middle Eight**: score late in the 2nd quarter, get the ball right back after halftime, and score again before the other team touches it.

So I tested it. I pulled every coin toss on record in this rivalry, rebuilt the opening drive of every game since 1998, and checked everything against 8,310 FBS games. Here are the answers up front:

<Question n={1} id="streaks" q="Is this rivalry streakier than chance?" a="Mostly no. One streak is real." />

The tie hides a long Bowling Green era. BG led the series by as many as 11 games after 1994, and Toledo needed 31 years to dig out.

<SeriesExplorer />

Rivalries get told as stories about streaks. To check whether this one really is streaky, I shuffled the order of the 86 decided games 20,000 times. Try a few shuffles yourself:

<StreakShuffle />

- **The series as a whole isn't streaky.** It has 40 runs of consecutive wins. Random orderings average 44, and about 1 in 4 is at least as streaky.
- **BG's 12 straight (1955 to 1966) is real.** Only 0.8% of shuffles produce a run that long. It covers Doyt Perry's entire tenure as BG's head coach.
- **Toledo's 9 straight (2010 to 2018) isn't unusual.** About 1 in 10 shuffles has a run that long.

Home field helps less than you'd think. Home teams are 48-38 in decided games (55.8%), which over 86 games isn't statistically different from a coin flip (p = 0.33).

<Question n={2} id="toss" q="What do captains choose, and does it work?" a="They defer 22 of 26 times. Toss winners are 13-13." />

Official box scores record the toss for 26 of the 28 games since 1998. Every line below is copied word for word, typos included, with a link to its source.

<CoinTossLedger />

- **Four times the winner took the ball:** Bowling Green in 2004, 2007 and 2015, and Toledo in 2009.
- **Who defers doesn't matter either.** Toledo is 7-3 when it deferred and 7-5 when Bowling Green deferred (p = 0.68).
- **Two games can't be coded.** The 1998 line says BG deferred and *"Miami will receive."* Miami wasn't playing, and the kickoffs show the opposite of what the line says. The 2000 box score has no play-by-play.

<Question n={3} id="opening" q="Does the team that gets the ball first play it safe, or make a statement?" a="Neither. Opening drives look the same no matter who deferred." />

Deferring hands the other team the opening possession. One theory says that team plays it safe. Another says it comes out swinging to send a message. I rebuilt the first drive of all 27 games since 1998 from the official play-by-play to find out.

<OpeningDrives />

- **Nobody plays it safe.** When Toledo deferred, Bowling Green scored on the opening drive 5 of 10 times. When Bowling Green deferred, Toledo scored 5 of 12 times. Same rate (p = 1.0).
- **Nobody makes a statement either.** 17 of 27 opening snaps were runs. The two biggest first snaps were BG's 56-yard run in 2019 and Toledo's 39-yard pass in 2025.
- **Taking the ball didn't pay.** The four teams that won the toss and took the ball scored once. Bowling Green threw an interception on its very first snap in 2015.

What does show up is a fast start. Teams that scored on the opening drive won 7 of 11 games. Teams that didn't won 4 of 16. That's suggestive (p = 0.06), not proof, and it probably reflects which team was better that night more than anything about the coin.

<Question n={4} id="sample" q="Doesn't 16-11 for the 2nd-half receiver mean something?" a="No. A fair coin gets there 44% of the time." />

The team that receives the 2nd-half kickoff, usually the one that deferred, is 16-11 in this rivalry (59.3%). Before you call that an edge, see how often a worthless one produces the same record:

<TwentySevenCoins />

The 95% interval on 16-11 runs from 41% to 76%. With 27 games, only an effect of about 27 percentage points would reliably show up, and no coin toss is worth that. Even if the league's tiny point estimate were real, you'd need about **78,400** rivalry games to see it. At one game a year, check back around the year 80,000.

Others have looked at this, mostly in the NFL. Before deferring was allowed, NFL teams that received the opening kickoff won 51.2% of 2,110 games ([numberFire](https://m.numberfire.com/nfl/news/7416/should-nfl-teams-defer-after-winning-the-coin-toss)). ESPN found no advantage to getting the ball to start the second half ([FootballScoop](https://footballscoop.com/news/number-nfl-coaches-deferring-kickoff-skyrocketed-past-decade)). Sharp Football found NFL teams that receive the second-half kickoff score about 1.6 more second-half points ([Sharp Football Analysis](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/analysis/opening-kickoff-receive-or-defer/)). In college, the stat everyone quotes is that Middle Eight winners won 76% of FBS games from 2014 to 2019 ([example](https://2201kimball.substack.com/p/the-middle-eight)). That's a correlation. Good teams win the Middle Eight, and good teams win games. The coin toss lets you separate the two.

So I went to a sample that *can* answer it: every regular-season FBS vs. FBS game from 2014 to 2025 in the CollegeFootballData.com database. A coin decides who gets the 2nd-half kickoff, so this is close to a natural experiment. The receivers look like a random half of all teams: their average closing spread is -0.22 points, and they're at home 49.2% of the time.

<Question n={5} id="middle-eight" q="So does deferring help at all?" a="For about eight minutes. Then it's gone." />

<MiddleEightVsGame />

- **It wins the Middle Eight.** The 2nd-half receiver outscores its opponent by **+0.93 points** in the last 4:00 of the 2nd quarter plus the first 4:00 of the 3rd (95% CI +0.75 to +1.11, 5,545 games).
- **It doesn't win the game.** Adjusted for the closing spread, receiving the 2nd half changes win probability by **-0.15 percentage points** (95% CI -1.45 to +1.16). Against the spread it's -0.17 points.
- **It holds every season.** The 2nd-half receiver's win rate runs from 46.9% to 53.7% with no trend. Dropping 2020 gives 50.2%.

Why doesn't the edge carry through? My best guess: receiving the 2nd half means kicking off in the 1st, so the other team gets the game's first possession. Over 60 minutes, the possessions even out. I haven't tested that directly yet.

> Deferring buys you about one point, for about eight minutes. Then the game takes it back.

## Data and method

Everything here is reproducible. The [repo](https://github.com/aahmadf123/battle-of-I75) has the code, the coin-toss and opening-drive tables with source links, and a full methodology, including every place the official records disagree with each other (there are eight).

- **Series results:** Toledo and BGSU athletics series pages, reconciled with CollegeFootballData.com by majority vote.
- **Coin tosses, opening drives and weather:** official box scores and play-by-play from utrockets.com and bgsufalcons.com.
- **League data:** CollegeFootballData.com games, closing lines and drives.

I work with the Toledo Athletics Data Science and Sports Analytics Department. This piece uses only public data and reflects my own views, not the university's.
