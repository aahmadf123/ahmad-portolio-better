# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Three overlapping audiences, all arriving with high intent:

- **Tech recruiters and hiring managers** at AI/ML-forward companies, scanning for demonstrated depth (not credentials). They skim fast and read closely only when something hooks them.
- **Research collaborators and professors** evaluating fit for joint work, grants, or co-authorship. They want rigor, range, and evidence of independent thinking.
- **Potential clients and companies** considering contract or consulting engagements. They need confidence that Ahmad can own a problem end-to-end, not just contribute to one.

Common thread: all three are time-constrained and skeptical of self-promotion. The work has to speak louder than the words.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for Ahmad Firas — Computer Science & Engineering graduate, AI Researcher (LION Lab, CPHS Lab, University of Toledo), and Microsoft Solution Developer. The site exists to create engagement across three channels simultaneously: job placement, research collaboration, and client work.

Success looks like: a visitor spending more than 90 seconds on the page, clicking into at least one project or research item, and leaving with a clear picture of what Ahmad can do and why it matters.

## Brand Personality

Precise, confident, curious.

Tone is direct and technical without being cold. Confidence from demonstrated outcomes (specific metrics, published research, shipped systems), not from self-description. Curiosity shows in the range of work and the willingness to move across domains: UAV autonomy, enterprise AI, sports analytics, database engineering.

The emotional register is: "this person has thought hard about real problems and can show you the receipts."

## Anti-references

Avoid anything that reads as AI-generated or template-assembled:

- Generic dark-tech SaaS aesthetics (neon purple, glass cards stacked on a gradient blob background)
- Portfolios built from a Webflow or Framer template with the same hero-metric layout everyone uses
- Any design where you could swap the name and domain and it would still make sense
- Excessive motion that signals "impressive demo" over "thoughtful communication"
- Buzzword-heavy copy that summarizes skills instead of showing outcomes

The site should feel made by one specific person for a specific purpose — not generated.

## Design Principles

1. **Specificity earns credibility.** Numbers, metrics, and real outcomes (72.8% zero-shot UAV deployment, R² = 0.976, 97.3% HITL success) matter more than any adjective. Every design decision should create space for the specific, not bury it in generic framing.

2. **Precision over polish.** The engineering mindset should be legible in the design: tight, deliberate, no wasted elements. The site should feel built by someone who thinks carefully, not assembled from components.

3. **Range without fragmentation.** The breadth of work (autonomous drones, agentic AI, sports analytics, MLOps, database engineering) is the differentiator. Surfaces it as connected thinking, not a scattered skill list.

4. **Transparent navigation, always.** Consistent with Ahmad's human-in-the-loop research ethos: users should always know where they are, what they're looking at, and what to do next. No dead ends, no mystery sections.

5. **Presence from subject matter, not aesthetics.** The space/cosmos visual language should feel earned by the domain (autonomous systems, AI research, deep computation) — not adopted as a style. If the visual choices could belong to any dark-themed tech site, they need rethinking.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No specific WCAG level required. `prefers-reduced-motion` is already respected in the codebase (all transitions and reveals disable under the media query). Maintain that coverage for any new additions.
